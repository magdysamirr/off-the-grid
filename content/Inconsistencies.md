An inconsistency is a mismatch: ideas, statements, arguments, or practices do not agree with one another. It can also mean behaviour or quality that does not stay the same across situations. [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/inconsistency)

In operations, an inconsistency may be a policy applied differently by different people, a report that uses changing definitions, or a gap between stated procedure and actual practice. Unlike a strict contradiction, it may not make two claims logically impossible. It still matters when the variation is unexplained or unfair.

Compare like with like: same rule, situation, period, and relevant conditions. Then determine whether the difference is justified adaptation, a recording problem, or an execution gap.
