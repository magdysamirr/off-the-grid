Working with a partner who thinks differently costs something. What's obvious to me needs an argument. What's obvious to him just arrives, and I can't check where it came from. That friction never goes away. It's the same difference that makes the partnership valuable.

But the payoff isn't a smoother relationship. It's coverage.

I check work by asking if it fits the architecture. That's my whole method, and it means I stop seeing details to see the shape. My partner reads the same work line by line. He catches the sentence that will upset a specific client, because of something that happened in a meeting eight months ago. I've shipped work that was structurally sound but flawed, more than once. He found the flaw in four minutes, every time.

When two people have different blind spots, the company isn't exposed at just one point. Most of what I know about my own limits, I learned by watching him succeed at something I had decided didn't matter.
