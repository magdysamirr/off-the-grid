We try to refuse work that doesn't build a position. When we can't walk away from it, we refuse to own the result instead.

Most agencies take any client who can pay, then try to make the deliverables good enough to matter. We filter before that: if a client doesn't have, or won't build, something competitors can't easily copy, we say so and try to turn the work down.

Sometimes we take it anyway, a relationship, a slow month, a client who insists. When that happens, we say it up front: we are not responsible for results built on a position that doesn't exist. The execution can still be competent. It cannot compound, and we won't pretend otherwise to protect the invoice.

The reason we filter at all is simple. Work that isn't building a position can't [[Building Unfair Advantages|compound]], and taking it without saying so makes the client forgettable and our own portfolio weaker, at the same time, for the same fee.

One version of this in practice: a dental clinic asked us for general posting, the same content calendar any clinic in the area could run. We picked a different direction instead: build the doctor's LinkedIn on his own principles and mindset, not clinical technique, so it reads as a person instead of another practice's before-and-after feed. It was slower to produce and harder to explain than "just post more." A year in, competitors are still competing with each other on the same technique content. He's the only one in the market whose voice sounds like his own.

We don't market. We mark.
