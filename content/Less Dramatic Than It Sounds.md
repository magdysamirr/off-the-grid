Twenty-four hours. Ten of them are work. Seven are sleep. That leaves seven, and most days I don't plan them, I just move through them.

Chess some days. Vibe coding other days, building something nobody asked for because the shape of it is stuck in my head. Doodling when my hands need something to do while my brain is still on a client problem. A podcast or an educational video while I'm doing something else entirely. Swimming, because the sea is 200 meters away and there's no good reason not to.

None of it is scheduled. I hop between them through the week the way the mood and the energy allow, not the way a productivity post would tell you to. [[Structural Decisions vs Motivational Ones|The structure is in the work]]. The rest of the day doesn't need one.

## What actually costs something

The story people expect is dramatic: the internet drops, the power dies, you're cut off. It isn't that. Power cuts happen, but rarely longer than fifteen minutes. The internet is fine.

What actually costs something is duller. Water is scarce here, though it has nothing to do with the work, it's just a fact of the desert. Daily supplies cost more than they would in Alexandria. Services are fewer, so errands take longer than they should. None of it is a crisis. All of it adds friction to a day that would otherwise be simple.

![A page of real doodles from my notebook: perspective sketches of the clinic, texture studies, cube shading practice, and a few pen drawings done between other things](./static/notes/doodles-collage.webp)

## The part that surprised me

I expected off-grid to feel like a trade: give up convenience, get back time. That part's true. But the time doesn't arrive labeled as bonus hours for deep thinking. It arrives as an ordinary evening, and what I do with it is close to what anyone does with a free evening anywhere. I just have more of them, and fewer things pulling me out of the house to spend them on.
