[[Strategy]] is winning before you start. If you enter a fair fight, you've already failed strategically.

Michael Porter said it clearly: "The essence of strategy is choosing what NOT to do." Strategy means creating unfair advantages, choosing your battlefield, and being different, not just better.

Three questions expose whether you have strategy or just tactics:  
1. **Where will we play?** (Which battlefield?)
2. **How will we win?** (What's our advantage?)
3. **What will we NOT do?** (What will we sacrifice?)

The test: If your competitor can do exactly what you're doing, that's not strategy. That's keeping up.

## Six Strategic Patterns That Actually Work

### 1. Flanking: Attack Where They're Weak

**Example: Dollar Shave Club vs. Gillette (2012)**  
Gillette owned 70%+ market share, spent billions on TV ads with athletes, and controlled retail shelf space. Dollar Shave Club didn't fight there.

**Their flank:**  
- Made a $4,500 YouTube video instead of million-dollar TV spots
- Used humor and directness instead of serious athlete endorsements
- Sold subscriptions online instead of competing for retail shelf space
- Positioned on "good enough quality at great price" instead of "best technology"

**Trade-off:** Gave up prestige positioning and retail distribution entirely.

**Result:** 12,000 orders in 48 hours, 26 million YouTube views, sold to Unilever for $1 billion in 2016.

[Watch the original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZUG9qYTJMsI) | [Read the case study](https://bretwaters.medium.com/case-study-dollar-shave-club-a9503fc83ea0)

**Lesson:** They attacked where Gillette was weak: ***price***, accessibility, tone, not where Gillette was strong.

---

### 2. New Territory: Create Category You Own

**Example: Red Bull Stratos (2012)**  
Energy drink market was crowded. Everyone competed on "our drink gives you energy." Red Bull stopped competing there entirely.

**Their new territory:**  
- Funded Felix Baumgartner to jump from edge of space (24 miles up)
- Live-streamed on YouTube, not TV
- Created actual entertainment, not drink advertising
- Associated brand with impossible human achievement

**Trade-off:** Spent $50M+ on single event instead of spreading across many ads. Didn't mention drink features at all.

**Result:** 8 million concurrent viewers, $100M+ earned media, 52M+ YouTube views. "Red Bull gives you wings" permanently linked to extreme achievement.

[Watch the space jump](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FHtvDA0W34I) | [Read the analysis](https://thebrandhopper.com/2024/02/05/a-case-study-on-red-bull-stratos-campaign/)

**Lesson:** Created new category: *media company producing extreme content*. Owned territory competitors didn't know existed.

---

### 3. Concentration: Overwhelming Force at One Point

**Example: Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (2010)**  
Old Spice was "grandfather's deodorant." Young men bought Axe instead. Traditional ads weren't working.

**Their concentration:**  
- Targeted WOMEN (who buy 60% of men's body wash), not men
- ONE video format: Isaiah Mustafa talking to "ladies"
- ONE week ALL resources: 186 personalized response videos to fans/celebrities
- ONE message: "Your man could smell like this"

**Trade-off:** Ignored men entirely. Abandoned traditional masculine advertising. Bet everything on viral moment, not sustained TV campaign.

**Result:** 60M+ views, sales increased 125% in one year, became #1 body wash for men in U.S.

[Watch the original](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=owGykVbfgUE) | [See response videos](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLB77114449B83E6F4)

**Lesson:** Overwhelming force at one point in time beats moderate effort everywhere.

### 4. Redirection: Use Their Strength Against Them

**Example: Burger King "Whopper Detour" (2018)**  
Burger King has fewer locations than McDonald's. Can't compete on convenience. Instead of fighting that weakness, they weaponized it.

**Their redirection:**  
- App promotion: "Get Whopper for 1 cent... ONLY within 600 feet of McDonald's"
- Forced customers to go to McDonald's, open BK app, then leave for BK
- Made McDonald's locations trigger for thinking about Burger King
- Turned McDonald's biggest advantage (more locations) into BK's marketing billboards

**Trade-off:** Complicated user journey. Required going to competitor first. Limited time (9 days only).

**Result:** BK app became #1 downloaded (jumped from 686th place), 1.5 million downloads in 9 days, 3B+ earned media impressions, 37:1 ROI.

[Watch the campaign](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KfNJXLS-KeQ) | [Read the breakdown](https://thebrandhopper.com/2024/08/18/a-case-study-on-burger-kings-whopper-detour-campaign/)

**Lesson:** Aikido marketing. Every McDonald's became BK advertisement. Turned strength (more locations) into weakness.

### 5. Vertical Integration: Own the Entire Journey

**Example: Glossier (2014-Present)**  
Beauty industry dominated by L'Oréal, Estée Lauder with billions in ads and retail presence. Traditional model: brand → advertising → retail → customer. High costs, retail markup, no customer data.

**Their integration:**  
- Started with blog "Into The Gloss" (owned audience before selling anything)
- Asked readers what products they wanted (owned customer insight)
- Created products from community feedback (owned product development)
- Sold directly through website only (owned sales data)
- Encouraged user photos (owned content creation)

**Customer journey they control:** Blog reader → community member → product co-creator → customer → advocate → content creator → recruits new readers.

**Trade-off:** No retail presence (gave up impulse purchases). Slower initial growth. Had to build audience from zero.

**Result:** $100M revenue in 4 years with minimal paid ads, valued at $1.2 billion by 2019, 70%+ purchases from peer recommendations.

[Visit Glossier](https://www.glossier.com) | [Read the Harvard case](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=54905)

**Lesson:** Like Rockefeller owned oil from well to gas station, Glossier owned beauty from blog to checkout to advocacy.

---

### 6. Trojan Horse: Make Customers Do the Marketing

**Example: Spotify Wrapped (2016-Present)**  
Music streaming is commoditized, everyone has same songs. Hard to differentiate. Users don't actively promote streaming services.

**Their trojan horse:**  
- Created personalized year-end summary (top songs, artists, minutes)
- Made it shareable social graphics
- Released before holidays (peak social sharing time)
- Made it feel like personal achievement ("I'm top 1% Harry Styles listener")

**The trick:** Users don't feel like advertisers. They feel like they're sharing about THEMSELVES. But every share is Spotify ad with personal endorsement. Each share recruits friends who want THEIR wrapped → app downloads.

**Trade-off:** Only works once a year. Requires significant data infrastructure. Some embarrassing data (but that's the fun).

**Result:** 156M+ users engaged with Wrapped 2023. Dominated Twitter/Instagram entire week in December. Billions of impressions, all organic.

[See Wrapped](https://www.spotify.com/wrapped/) | [Read the analysis](https://thebrandhopper.com/2025/06/10/a-case-study-on-spotify-wrapped-the-storytelling-phenomenon/)

**Lesson:** Users brought advertising inside their social feeds willingly. Users are the horse. Social media is Troy.

## How to Apply This

### Before Every Campaign, Ask:

**1. Which strategic pattern are we using?**  
- Flanking (attack where competitor is weak)
- New Territory (create category no one owns)
- Concentration (overwhelming force at one point)
- Redirection (use their strength against them)
- Integration (own entire customer journey)
- Trojan Horse (customers do the marketing)

**2. What's our unfair advantage?** First-mover? Network effects? Content moat? Technology? Community? If you don't have one, you're not being strategic.

**3. What are we choosing NOT to do?** No trade-offs = no strategy. Must sacrifice 100 good options for 1 great one.

**4. Can competitors easily copy this?** If yes → not strategic. If no → potentially strategic.

**5. Does this compound over time?** Tactics deplete (discount ends). Strategy compounds (reputation grows, content accumulates, network strengthens).

---

## Final Thought  
Tactics are what you do. Strategy is WHY you do it THERE, THAT WAY, and not somewhere else.  
Everyone has tactics. Few have strategy.  
We don't market. We mark.

Common thread: All had CLEAR strategy before execution. None were "let's post and see what happens."
