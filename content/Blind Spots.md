A blind spot is an area a person cannot see or understand well. In management, it is an important part of the work that remains unobserved, unasked about, or outside the current model.

The Cambridge Dictionary uses the term for an area someone cannot see, or a subject they find difficult to understand. [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/blind-spot)

For my system, a blind spot is not simply missing data. It is a consequential gap in observation: a measure nobody owns, a dependency no one checks, or a question the current reporting never asks.

The useful test is: **What important thing could be going on here that our current sources and checks would not reveal?**

Blind spots are often discovered by comparing what the system claims to cover with the real process, asking people close to the work, or noticing an outcome that the current explanation cannot account for. Mark the evidence and uncertainty. A suspected blind spot is a prompt to investigate, not proof of failure.
